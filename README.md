## About

I am a Biomedical Engineering student at King's College London working at the intersection of brain–computer interfaces (BCIs), machine learning, and neural engineering.

My research focuses on non-invasive inner-speech decoding and assistive communication, spanning EEG-based neural signal translation and sEMG-based silent-speech interfaces. I am particularly interested in developing end-to-end systems that combine deep learning, biosignal processing, and custom embedded hardware to translate physiological signals into meaningful communication.

## Research Projects

### 1. EEG-Based Inner-Speech Translation

**IEEE Conference Publication · 2026**

Developed a machine learning architecture for non-invasive EEG-based inner-speech translation, investigating its potential for assistive communication in ALS.

- Designed a region-aware neural architecture combining specialised cortical encoders, cross-region feature fusion, and a Transformer decoder.
- Applied LoRA/PEFT fine-tuning to Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, with cosine-similarity and orthogonalization regularisation.
- Evaluated performance on the Chisco imagined-speech dataset, achieving a BLEU-1 score of **0.512 ± 0.012** and a ROUGE-L F1 score of **0.396 ± 0.009**.

**Publication:** [A Novel Machine Learning Model for Non-Invasive EEG-Based Inner-Speech Translation in ALS](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11435085/)

### 2. sEMG-Based Inner-Speech Translation

**Independent Research & Hardware Development · 2024–Present**

Developing a wearable sEMG-based system for silent-speech decoding, with the goal of enabling discreet, non-audible communication for individuals with speech impairments.

- Developed a multimodal generative architecture integrating region-aware convolutional attention and a Transformer decoder.
- Designed custom PCBs and surface electromyography electrodes for biosignal acquisition.
- Built a synchronised data acquisition and labelling application to support dataset development and model training.
- Secured **$2,500 in funding** for research and hardware development.
